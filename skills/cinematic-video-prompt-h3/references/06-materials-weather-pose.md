# Materials, Weather & Pose

> **H3 mapping note (read first).** Three sub-vocabularies, all folded into H3 shot descriptions:
>
> 1. **Materials & textures** → adjectives or short clauses attached to the subject or a prop: `a velvet dress with crushed velvet texture absorbing the light,` / `a hammered-metal pendant catching the rim light,` / `wet asphalt reflections,`.
> 2. **Weather & atmosphere** → paired with **lighting** and **time of day** in the same shot. In H3: `… at night under heavy downpour, neon shop signs reflecting on wet asphalt.` Mismatched combos (`golden hour` + `heavy downpour`) will read literally. Section B of this reference is the source.
> 3. **Pose** → for characters, fold into the subject description. H3 reads `the young woman in a hands-clasped-anxiety pose, head bowed,` as well as any comma-separated form. Sequence emotional close-ups explicitly (see `SKILL.md` section 8.6).
>
> **Cross-link for facial expression.** This reference covers **body** pose. For facial expression on emotional close-ups, use FACS Action Units from `references/07-facs.md` — bare words like `sad face` or `angry expression` average into a generic grimace, while named AUs (`AU1 + AU2 + AU5 + AU26` for surprise, etc.) render the actual muscle movements. The two combine: face AUs for close-up, body pose for medium and wide shots.
>
> Two reminders specific to H3:
>
> - **Do not name a real person, studio, or character with a strong existing IP.** H3 may render toward that IP. Use a description of your own design (`SKILL.md` section 8.15).
> - **For exotic subjects (rare animals, fantasy creatures, named plants), describe the parts**, not the noun. A bare `gecko` tends to render as a generic lizard.
