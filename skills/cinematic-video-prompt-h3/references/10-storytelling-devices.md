# Storytelling Devices — Structure, Plot & Narrative Framing

> **H3 mapping note (read first).** Storytelling devices mostly affect the **structure of a sequence**, not the contents of an individual shot. They live in three places:
>
> 1. **`summary` (Ref2VA only)** — when the sequence uses parallel editing, a frame narrative, or non-linear structure, the summary describes the architecture in one short English paragraph.
> 2. **Shot-body sequencing** — flashback / flashforward / in medias res / cliffhanger require specific opening shots. State the structure once at the start of `[Shot 1]`.
> 3. **Cut timing** — foreshadowing, plant-and-payoff, and dramatic irony rely on what the audience sees **when**. Use `At MM:SS.mmm,` to plant and reveal at the right beats.
>
> For multi-shot H3 sequences (Ref2VA or chained T2VA clips), the storytelling device is the spine. Pair this reference with `references/08-editing-transitions.md` for parallel editing and montage.

The vocabulary of how stories are told — structure (three-act, in medias res, frame narrative), devices (flashback, foreshadowing, cliffhanger), and meta-techniques (dramatic irony, breaking fourth wall, voiceover). Loads of overlap with `references/09-sound-design.md` (voiceover narration, leitmotifs) and `references/08-editing-transitions.md` (parallel editing, montage).

## A. Story structure

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Three-Act Structure | Setup → confrontation → resolution | establish the act boundary in `[Shot 1]`: `[Shot 1] … the inciting incident, …` then escalate in subsequent shots |
| Inciting Incident | Event disrupting the protagonist's normal life | `[Shot 1] … the moment the door opens, the protagonist's life is upended.` |
| Character Arc | Protagonist's transformation through the story | repeat the character tag with evolving descriptors across shots: `the young woman, now resolute, …` vs the earlier `the young woman, hesitant, …` |
| Subplot | Secondary storyline alongside the main plot | label the subplot in `summary` if Ref2VA, or in `overall_soundscape` if a single motif |
| Plot / Story / Discourse | What happens, **what** the audience sees, **how** it is presented | shot-body sequencing is the discourse layer; plot lives in `summary` |

## B. Time and order

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Chronological Order | Events in time order | the default; no annotation needed |
| In Medias Res | Begin in the middle of the action | `[Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, … in the middle of a chase, the protagonist already running, the context to be filled in.` |
| Flashback | Cut to events from the past | `[Shot 2] At MM:SS.mmm, the shot cuts to a flashback — the same corridor twenty years earlier, the wallpaper brighter, the woman younger, the air softer.` |
| Flashforward | Cut to events from the future | same pattern as flashback, with future cues: `the future version of herself, the dress stained, the ring missing.` |
| Non-Linear Narrative | Events presented out of chronological order | describe the timeline structure in `summary` (Ref2VA): `the sequence begins in the present, flashes back to childhood, returns to the present with new understanding.` |
| Frame Narrative | One story nested inside another | label both stories in `summary`: `an old man (outer) tells the story of his youth (inner); both unfold in parallel shots.` |
| Parallel Storylines | Multiple narratives eventually converging | label in `summary`; use parallel editing (`references/08`) to intercut them |
| Voiceover Narration | Spoken commentary over images | `<d>[English] ...</d> while her lips remain completely closed.` (see `references/09-sound-design.md` for full rules) |
| Flash-Cut | Very brief flash of an image, often a memory or premonition | `[Shot 1] … at MM:SS.mmm, a flash-cut of the accident lasts a fraction of a second, then returns to the present.` |
| Slow Time | Time moves perceptibly slower than real (no slow-motion FX) | describe the subjective sense: `time stretches — every breath audible, every footstep a slow weight.` |

## C. Plot devices

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Foreshadowing | Plant a hint about a later event | `[Shot 1] … a single red thread hanging from her collar, unnoticed.` then revisit in a later shot |
| Plant & Payoff | Establish an element early (plant), make it significant later (payoff) | label in `summary` if multi-shot: `the red thread planted in Shot 1 becomes the binding in Shot 5.` |
| MacGuffin | Plot device that motivates characters, has no intrinsic importance | describe the object once and keep it present in motive shots: `the briefcase, its contents never shown, drives every decision.` |
| Red Herring | Misleading element to divert attention | pair with foreshadowing to deliberately mislead: `the butler is shown holding the knife — the obvious answer, deliberately wrong.` |
| Chekhov's Gun | An element introduced early must be used later | implicit in plant-and-payoff; just make sure the planted object appears again |
| Cliffhanger | End at a moment of unresolved suspense | end the last shot mid-event: `[Shot N] … at MM:SS.mmm, the door opens; the shot ends on her reaction, just as the figure is revealed.` |
| Deus Ex Machina | Sudden, unearned resolution | rarely a deliberate H3 choice; if used, signal it explicitly: `an impossible rescue, signaled by the abrupt warm light flooding in.` |
| Twist Ending | Final revelation recontextualizes earlier shots | state the twist in the final shot's description and ensure the earlier shots have the supporting detail |

## D. Point of view

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| First Person | Story told from "I" | pair with voiceover narration: `<d>[English] I still remember that road.</d> while her lips remain completely closed.` |
| Limited Third | Story from one character's perspective | keep the camera on or near that character; use POV only for them |
| Omniscient | Story knows all characters' thoughts | state it explicitly in `summary` (Ref2VA): `the camera shifts between the three characters' perspectives, none privileged.` |
| Unreliable Narrator | Narrator whose account cannot be trusted | describe the discrepancy: `her voiceover describes a happy childhood, while the visuals show an empty house.` |
| Breaking Fourth Wall | Character addresses the audience | `<d>[English] You see, you were right about me all along.</d>` directed at the lens |
| Direct Address | Speaking directly to camera without breaking the fourth wall (e.g., vlog, training) | `a woman in a navy blazer speaks directly to camera, eye contact with the lens, …` |

## E. Symbolism and theme

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Symbolism | Objects representing abstract ideas | describe the symbol once and return to it: `a single white moth on her shoulder — returning in the final shot, wings now folded.` |
| Motif | Recurring element that develops meaning through repetition | pair with leitmotif in `references/09-sound-design.md` for audio motifs |
| Visual Metaphor | One image standing for another idea | describe the metaphor directly: `her reflection in the window shows a younger version of herself, layered over the present.` |
| Juxtaposition | Two ideas set side by side for contrast | cut them in alternation: `[Shot 1] the crowded party, …`, `[Shot 2] the empty room she just left.` |
| Dramatic Irony | Audience knows something characters don't | state both in `summary`: `the audience sees the gun; the character does not.` |
| Theme | Underlying idea; not directly annotated | implied by accumulated motifs, palette, and pacing |

## F. Narrative rhythm

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Rising Action | Tension escalates through the sequence | describe escalation in pacing: `[Shot 1] a slow establishing shot, …`, `[Shot 5] a fast handheld close-up, …` |
| Climax | Peak moment of tension or emotion | use a clear visual change: `at MM:SS.mmm, the camera cuts to the tightest framing of the entire sequence — her face, eyes to chin.` |
| Falling Action | Tension releases after climax | describe the release: `the camera dollies out with small amplitude at slow speed, the corridor re-asserting itself.` |
| Resolution | Final state | the last shot closes the loop: `the same door, now closed; the same corridor, now empty.` |
| Pacing | Speed of cutting across the sequence | state the rhythm explicitly: `the sequence cuts every 4 seconds — slow, deliberate — until the final 6 seconds, when cuts arrive every 1.5 seconds.` |
| Beats | Smallest units of action within a shot | covered in main SKILL section 8.1; sequence 3–5 beats per shot |

## G. Failure modes for H3

- **G.1 — Structure not stated.** A "flashback" shot that does not signal when in the timeline it lives reads as a continuation of the present. Use `the shot cuts to a flashback —` or `a younger version of herself, twenty years earlier, …`.
- **G.2 — Cliffhanger without payoff.** If the brief calls for a cliffhanger, end the **last shot** on the unresolved moment; do not cut to resolution.
- **G.3 — Voiceover that mumbles.** State the voiceover text verbatim. Never `she says something reflective`; write the actual `<d>` text.
- **G.4 — POV without consent.** Mark POV as a camera motion (`POV,`), not as a label. A "POV" tag in the description without the motion verb reads as nothing.
- **G.5 — Plant-and-payoff buried.** If you plant an object in Shot 1, mention it in the relevant later shot by the same name. `the red thread` in Shot 1 must become `the same red thread` in Shot 5.
- **G.6 — Frame narrative confusion.** If the outer story is the present and the inner is the past, label them in `summary` and use a consistent visual distinction (color, grain, aspect ratio) for the inner.
- **G.7 — Symbol overload.** One symbol per shot reads as deliberate; four reads as a crossword puzzle. Cap at one visual symbol + one audio motif per shot.
