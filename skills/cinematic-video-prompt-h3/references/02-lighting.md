# Lighting

> **H3 mapping note (read first).** H3 reads lighting as inline adjectives or short sentences **inside** `[Shot 1] …` (or `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, …`). Pick 1–2 keywords per shot from this reference and fold them into the shot description. Common patterns:
>
> - Adjective form (placed after the subject): `a rim-lit young woman …`, `a chiaroscuro-lit corridor …`, `a soft-window-lit café table …`.
> - Sentence form (preferred when lighting is a key dramatic element): `Lighting comes from a single candle at her chest; the rest of the corridor falls into deep shadow.`
> - Source + quality: pair lighting with its **source** (window, candle, neon sign, overhead sun) and its **quality** (soft, hard, dappled, volumetric).
>
> Lighting **must match** the weather and time of day in the same shot. Mismatches like `golden hour` + `heavy downpour` will be read literally and look wrong.

## B1. Lighting Setup Vocabulary — The Basics

> **Heuristic.** Cinematographers describe a lighting setup by listing the **named roles** of each light. The model doesn't need every term; one or two well-chosen role names carry the look.

| Term | Role | Visible result | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key Light | The primary light on the subject — usually the brightest, defines shape | `the key light from camera left sculpts her cheekbone, …` |
| Fill Light | Softens the shadow side of the subject | `a soft fill light lifts the shadow side of her face, …` |
| Back Light / Rim Light | Light from behind, separating subject from background | `a back light edges her hair against the dark wall, …` |
| Three-Point Lighting | Standard key + fill + back combination | `three-point lighting — key from camera left, fill at half intensity, back rim, …` |
| Two-Point Lighting | Key + back (no fill, for higher contrast) | `two-point lighting — a hard key and a rim, the shadow side falling into deep shadow, …` |
| Practical Light / Practical | Visible light source on set (lamp, candle, neon) that also illuminates | `the practical desk lamp is the key — the warm glow falls on her hands, …` |
| Motivated Lighting | Light source is justified by something in the scene | `motivated lighting — the window is the key, the practical lamp a fill, the street lamp the rim, …` |
| Negative Fill | Black flag or dark surface *absorbing* light, deepening shadow | `negative fill on the shadow side keeps the cheek in deep shadow, …` |
| Bounce Light | Light reflected off a surface (wall, ceiling, reflector) | `a bounce card at camera-left lifts the shadow side with a soft wrap, …` |
| High-Key Lighting | Bright, low-contrast, few shadows — upbeat, comedic, beauty | `high-key lighting — bright, soft, low contrast; the face evenly lit with minimal shadow, …` |
| Low-Key Lighting | Dark, high contrast, deep shadow — drama, horror, thriller | `low-key lighting — a single key, deep shadow on the rest of the face, …` (already in section C) |
| Key-to-Fill Ratio | The brightness relationship between key and fill (2:1, 4:1, 8:1) | describe in words: `the key is roughly twice the fill — soft but with shape, …` / `the key is four times the fill — sculpted shadow, …` |
| Light Falloff | How quickly light drops off with distance | `the light falls off quickly — the foreground is bright, the background falls into shadow, …` |
| Spill Control / Flagging | Using flags or barn doors to prevent light spilling where it's not wanted | `a flag on the key prevents spill onto the background, …` |
| Light Source Quality | Hard vs soft — size of the source relative to subject | describe in words: `a hard sun throws sharp shadows, …` / `a soft overcast sky wraps the light around her face, …` |
| Color Temperature | Warm (2700K) vs cool (6500K+) | `a warm 3200K practical — the candle reads as candlelight amber, …` (already in section D / section A) |

Worked example, three-point setup named explicitly:

```
A soft window key from camera-left sculpts her cheekbone. Negative fill on
the opposite side keeps the shadow cheek dark. A dim practical lamp behind
her separates her hair from the background wall.
```

## Portrait Lighting Setups (named patterns)

These are established portrait-lighting recipes with specific shadow shapes. Use them when the shot is a face close-up and you want the light to do recognizable work.

| Pattern | Light position | Shadow shape on face | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rembrandt Lighting | Key 45° to one side, slightly above eye-level | Triangle of light on the shadow-side cheek | `Rembrandt lighting — key 45° to the side, the small triangle of light on the shadow cheek; the rest of the face falls into soft shadow.` |
| Loop Lighting | Key slightly to side and above eye-level | Small nose-shadow angled toward the mouth corner | `loop lighting — key slightly off-axis and above; a small shadow loops from the nose toward the cheek.` |
| Broad Lighting | Key illuminates the side of the face turned toward camera | Face appears wider; lit side dominant | `broad lighting — the camera-facing side of the face is lit, making the face appear wider.` |
| Short Lighting | Key illuminates the side turned away from camera | Camera-facing side in shadow; face appears narrower | `short lighting — the side turned away from camera is lit, the camera-facing side in shadow.` |
| Split Lighting | Key 90° to one side | Half the face fully lit, the other half in shadow | `split lighting — the face is divided exactly in half, one side lit, the other in complete shadow.` |
| Butterfly Lighting (a.k.a. Paramount) | Key above and in front of the subject | Small symmetrical shadow under the nose | `butterfly lighting — key directly above and in front; a symmetrical shadow sits beneath the nose.` |
| Clamshell Lighting | Key + fill from above and below the face, both frontal | Even frontal illumination with soft contour | `clamshell lighting — key from above, fill from below, both frontal; the face evenly lit with a soft contour.` |

## Lighting Direction & Source Vocabulary

| Term | Definition | H3 phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal Lighting | Key directly in front of subject, aligned with camera | `frontal lighting — the key is in front, aligned with the camera, …` |
| Top Lighting | Key directly overhead | `top lighting — the key from directly above, casting shadows downward on face and body, …` |
| Underwater Lighting | Blue-green diffused illumination, subaquatic feel | `underwater lighting — blue-green diffused, particles suspended, …` |
| Hair Light | Light from behind onto hair and shoulders, separating from background | `a hair light from behind picks out the rim of her hair, …` |
| Accent Light | Focused illumination on a specific area for emphasis | `an accent light picks out the locket at her throat, …` |
| Mood Lighting | Prioritizes emotional atmosphere over realism | `mood lighting — saturated amber, soft falloff, the realism sacrificed for atmosphere, …` |
| Firelight | Warm flickering orange from a real fire source | `firelight — warm flickering orange dancing across her face, the candles throwing moving shadows on the wall, …` |
| Neon Lighting | Vibrant glowing tubes or LED equivalents | `neon lighting — pink and cyan tubes casting saturated color on the wet pavement, …` |
| Day for Night | Filming in daylight to simulate night | `day for night — underexposed daylight grade with deep blue cast, …` |
| Night for Night | Filming real night scenes at night | `night for night — actual low-light capture, real city lights and darkness, …` |
| Diffusion | Softening harsh light through silk, muslin, frost | `diffused through silk, the key light wraps around her face, …` |
| Cucoloris (Cookie) | Patterned board between source and subject casting shaped shadows | `a cucoloris casting window-blind shadows on the wall behind her, …` |
